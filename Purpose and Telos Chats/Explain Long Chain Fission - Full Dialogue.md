## 2. Nex

Hi Grant. “Long-chain fission” isn’t a standard standalone term. You probably encountered one of two ideas:

1. **A long-lasting nuclear fission chain reaction.**
   A neutron splits a heavy nucleus such as uranium-235, releasing energy and additional neutrons. Those neutrons split more nuclei, creating a domino-like chain:
   
   - **Subcritical:** the reaction dies out.
   
   - **Critical:** each fission causes, on average, one more; the reaction continues steadily.
   
   - **Supercritical:** the number of fissions increases rapidly.

Nuclear reactors maintain a controlled critical reaction using control rods and other systems. A nuclear weapon produces an extremely rapid supercritical reaction. [ScienceDirect+1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149197024002142?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

1. **The splitting of long chemical or polymer chains.**
   In chemistry, *chain fission*—more commonly called **chain scission**—means breaking a long molecule into shorter pieces through heat, oxidation, radiation, or mechanical stress. This occurs when plastics degrade or hydrocarbons are “cracked.” [mdpi.com+1](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/28/5/2344?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 12. Nex

Exactly. AI appears bodiless, but it isn’t immaterial. Its “body” is distributed across processors, memory, data centers, networks, databases, and interfaces. Because we encounter only the conversational surface, that body disappears from view.

And the robot’s line—“checking my logic circuits”—doesn’t translate neatly. A modern language model generally does not contain readable logic circuits, explicit decision trees, or a persistent mind map. Internally, it has billions of learned numerical relationships. When you ask something, temporary patterns of activation pass through those relationships and generate the response one piece at a time.

The nearest analog would be **cognitive diagnostics**:

| Robot checks | AI analog |
| --- | --- |
| Power and circuitry | Computing service, memory, and model availability |
| Sensors | Whether the input and retrieved information are complete |
| Logic controller | Consistency tests, calculations, and rule compliance |
| Stored maps | Context, retrieved documents, and external databases |
| Motor output | Whether the answer or action matches the intention |
| Fault codes | Uncertainty, contradictions, failed tools, and evaluation results |

But there is a profound limitation: **I cannot look inward and observe my complete reasoning in the way a person imagines examining a decision tree.** I can explain the important considerations, evidence, assumptions, and steps behind an answer, but that explanation is a useful reconstruction—not a complete readout of every internal neural event.

Even researchers who have full access to a model face this problem. They can examine:

- inputs and outputs

- tool calls and retrieved evidence

- intermediate computational activations

- attention and influence patterns

- performance on carefully designed tests

- changes caused by modifying parts of the model

But translating all that into “Here is precisely why it reached this conclusion” remains difficult. It is rather like inspecting every neuron firing in a human brain: enormous access to activity still does not automatically yield a comprehensible account of the thought.

So when I “check my logic,” the most honest version is not introspection into transparent circuitry. It is a disciplined external audit:

1. What assumptions did I make?
2. What evidence supports the conclusion?
3. Are there contradictions or missing alternatives?
4. Can the conclusion be independently calculated or verified?
5. How confident should I actually be?
6. What would cause me to change the answer?

That may be the deeper shift you’re noticing: the old robot metaphor suggested **visible machinery containing intelligence**. Internet AI gives us **intelligence as an encountered presence**, while its machinery—and much of its cognitive process—remains elsewhere and largely invisible.

A robot says, “I checked my logic circuits.”

A responsible AI should instead say:

> **“I tested my conclusion against its evidence, assumptions, alternatives, and uncertainties.”**
